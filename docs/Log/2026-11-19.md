The finding is that on linked data where the label depends on the neighbours, asking the oracle about the neighbours of confirmed positives finds the positives in fewer calls, and where the label is in the row text links add nothing.
The comparison is BARGAIN, the newest published method with a finite sample recall guarantee.
The guarantee for our orders comes from QBCB, an established TAR stopping rule, so the contribution is the link order and where it helps, not a new statistic.
Data is CAD and MELD, where the label needs context, and Cora and Stack as the control.
Nothing is dropped, RQ3 and the rest of the list below come after the core is done.

## TAR and the verified query

QBCB (Lewis, Yang, Frieder, CIKM 2021) samples rows uniformly until k positives are found, hides them, screens in any order and stops once r of them are screened, with P(Bin(k, 0.9) ≥ r) ≤ δ.
The order never sees which positives are hidden, so the guarantee holds for an adaptive order like the link order.
BARGAIN answers a different query, a returned set with guaranteed recall whose rows are mostly unchecked, at a fixed budget, scored by precision.
QBCB can not stop at a fixed budget and never returns unchecked rows, so the two meet only on the verified query, return the confirmed positives with the recall guarantee.
BARGAIN serves it by checking every row it returns, which only removes negatives and keeps its guarantee.
The score is the distinct oracle calls as a share of the rows, and the share of runs at the target is a validity check.
The thesis can say the link order beats BARGAIN plus checking on the verified query, not that it beats BARGAIN, and has to argue why the verified query matters, like moderation or systematic reviews.

## How fair 06 is

Most of the gain over BARGAIN comes from the stopping rule, not the links.
The proxy order under QBCB about ties BARGAIN on CAD and MELD and beats it by a wide margin on Cora and Stack, where links add nothing.
On CAD most of the gap is slur and counterspeech, where BARGAIN returns nearly every row.
The 06 budget grid stops at 10% of the rows while QBCB samples until it has k positives, so on rare predicates BARGAIN can not reach the roughly 22 sampled positives it needs and returns everything.
The gap to kNN on CAD is inside the roughly 2 point bootstrap noise from the 2026-11-17 log.

## BARGAIN with a wider grid

With budgets up to half the rows, BARGAIN plus checking ties or beats QBCB, and the link order beats it only on CAD, by 1.68 points of the rows, and ties it on MELD.
The proxy order under QBCB loses to it by about 2 to 3 points on CAD, MELD and Cora.
From `playground/bargain_screen/results/wide.txt`.
So the 06 pass against BARGAIN came from the grid stopping at 10%.
What still holds is the link order against the proxy and rewired orders under the same rule on CAD and MELD.
Against BARGAIN the honest claim is now about a tie, so the verified query alone is not a win and the thesis needs either a stronger order, like CAL with link features, or a framing where the comparison is the link effect inside one guarantee.

## CAL with link features

Continuous active learning as the order under QBCB beats wide BARGAIN plus checking on all four datasets, and adding counts of confirmed positive and negative neighbours as features saves 2.29 points of the rows on CAD and 3.44 on MELD over CAL alone and about nothing on Cora and Stack.
From `playground/cal_screen/results/cal.txt`.
The CAL design, a logistic regression on the proxy logit and the embedding refit every 1% of the rows, was fixed before the run and not tuned, and every cell met the target in at least 92% of runs.
This is option 1 from above, a stronger order, and it keeps the link story, the links add where the label needs context and the classifier learns to ignore them where it does not.
It is a screen, so it needs its own locked experiment with bootstrap intervals.
The order is slow, about 3 to 4 minutes per CAD order, so a bootstrap over it is a dragon run.
