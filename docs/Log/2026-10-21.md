Ran proxy and cascade on both Stack edge sets.

## A trained classifier cannot catch the proxy either

Added a ceiling step, logistic regression on the embeddings scored out of fold, which bounds anything that learns from verdicts.
It beats embeddings on all 18 predicates but loses to the proxy on 9 of 11 on Stack and 6 of 7 on Cora.
So trick 1 from [learning from verdicts](../Ideas/Learning%20from%20verdicts.md) needs the proxy score as a feature, not embeddings alone.
Numbers are in `results/*/ceiling.txt`.

## Why the proxy is so close

Both models are Llama 3.1 with the same prompt, so the proxy is predicting what a bigger Llama says rather than the predicate.
On the 83 spss mentions the oracle calls no, the proxy also calls no on 73.
The keyword guess does not hold up, since a keyword predicate is where the 70B itself misses a third of the mentions.
What the proxy has is a good ranking, not good verdicts.
It calls yes on only 21% of the oracle's own positives among spss mentions, and the cascade only ever uses the score.

## Oracle error probably does not explain the link failure, but this is not settled

Took the union of the oracle labels and a keyword match, as an upper bound on how much the misses could matter, and reran the order.
Links get worse rather than better, and the lift drops every time.
interaction goes from 10.2x to 4.6x, robotics from 8.9x to 6.0x and proof from 4.5x to 1.3x.
So the lift is partly a property of the oracle's narrow label set and not of the graph.
The one large link win on Cora, robotics at 95%, goes from -11.8 to -1.1 under the union, so the -11.5 in the readme needs a caveat.

The correction is too crude to close the question.
A keyword is not a label, it over-adds on proof where it takes the rate to 28%, and on survey it catches a different set than the oracle so the rate barely moves.
markov is the case where the oracle is plainly wrong and also one of the few predicates where links did win, which is the opposite of what the crude test implies.
A blind read with controls is what would settle it.

## Why high lift gains nothing

Where an edge joins two positives the cosine already ranks that neighbour in the top half percent of the pool, median rank 0.1 to 0.5% over six predicates.
And fewer than half the Stack positives have a positive neighbour at all, 42 to 48%, so expansion runs out and the rest of the order is the random tie break.
That is why links alone sits next to random and adds nothing on top of embeddings.
The biggest all positive clump on interaction is 14 near duplicate questions about an interaction suppressing a main effect.

Meaning that the signal comming from links is duplicate signal from embeddings but weaker.
We either need to find an interesting way to combine them or find a dataset with better signal.

## The cosine graph beats postLinks

A kNN graph on the embeddings has higher lift and covers more positives than either real edge set, on all 10 real predicates, with fewer edges.
At 4,001 cosine edges against 5,653 postLinks and 5,665 same author, interaction is 14.6x against 10.2x and 3.0x, interpret 5.1x against 3.4x and 1.6x, spss 6.7x against 2.3x and 4.1x.
ownstudy is the author predicate and the author graph still loses, 2.4x against 2.8x.
Cora citations lose the same way, 14.6x against 9.0x on markov and 17.1x against 8.9x on robotics.
The only exception in the whole table is we, the control, on postLinks, which is the 2.2x graph baseline showing up again.
So both human edge sets are dominated by a graph that is free from the text I already have.
That is the reason question 2 died, and it is a cheap test to run before labelling anything next time.
An edge set is worth the work only if its lift and coverage beat a cosine kNN graph at the same density.

## Lift points the wrong way

The edges do not do a different job per predicate.
They encode topic once, and lift reports how topical the predicate is.
Spearman between cosine lift and the calls embeddings needs for 90% recall is -0.68 with p=0.02, and -0.56 with p=0.07 for postLinks.
So high lift means the text order already works, and links are only worth anything where it does not.
Lift was never going to be the cheap check for question 3, because it is a reading of the embedding and not of the graph.

## Other directions talked through

- Ask the LLM which tags or MeSH terms fit the predicate instead of asking about every row.
- Let my yes and no on a few rows define the predicate, and count reactions instead of oracle calls.
- Predicates whose answer depends on context outside the row, like the thread or the reviewed product, where links change the answer and not the cost.
- Use links and embeddings in some smart way.

The ceiling step says the 8B proxy already beats a fully supervised classifier on the embeddings, so there is no room under it to find.
The edges also have to carry something no human read off the rows, which rules out postLinks, and be dense enough to cover the positives, which is where same author failed at 44 to 65% of positives having no neighbour.
