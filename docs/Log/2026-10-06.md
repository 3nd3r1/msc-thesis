## Step 2

Ran it, numbers in the [readme](../../experiments/03_link_guided_search/README.md).
13,540 calls, not the 11,200 I estimated, since junk rows get labelled too.
Embedded the abstracts with MiniLM instead of Cora's bundled features, which are a bag of words picked for the 7 classes and predate the abstracts.
Raised seeds to 50, every predicate has fewer positives than step 1's noisiest class.

Labels came out clean.
The 12 repeated texts never got contradicting verdicts, and first person against a regex is 83% precision, which puts the pilot's 70 to 90% band on all 2,708 rows.

## The result

Embeddings beat links alone on all four real predicates.
Links plus embeddings ties on proof and biology, is worse on robotics, and only helps language.
The control is flat across every order, so this is a real negative and not a broken harness.

Links are not failing though.
58 to 81% of positives have a positive neighbour, robotics included, at a lift of 5 to 12x, the same range as step 1's classes.
The wording is just always tight, 1.6 to 2.5 times the corpus average, and the embedding reads the same words the oracle reads.
Step 1 worked because a class label is broad and its wording diffuse, which a specific predicate never is.

Also thought links plus embeddings was buggy, since one neighbour counts 1 against a cosine of 0.2, so links are a hard priority rather than a blend.
Wrote a weight and a sweep, then threw it away.
A weight fitted on the same labels the result is measured on is worse than what it fixes.

## Next

Not concluding this yet.
Two checks, an afternoon, no oracle calls, and they decide whether a good predicate exists at all.

- Scatter every term appearing in 2 to 15% of papers, linked against tight, with the 7 classes added as points. If the terms fall on one line and only the classes sit off it, the cell I want is empty on Cora and no predicate will find it.
- Rerun the embedding order on titles only against the labels I have. If embeddings degrade sharply while links hold, that is the example, and a realistic one, since DBLP has titles and citations but no abstracts.

What the thesis says either way.
If the thin text check shows a gap, links help when the text is thin, and testing it properly is a title only oracle, which is a cheap relabel.
If neither shows anything, the finding is that row links do carry signal but a good text embedding already contains it, step 1 is the case where it does not, and the adaptive rule is the method that does not lose.
That is a complete and honest result.
